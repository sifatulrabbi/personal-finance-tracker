# A debit card is a view onto its bank wallet

A debit card spends money that sits in a bank account, but the API let a debit-card wallet hold its own opening balance and activity with no link to that account. A household that added both the bank account and its card counted the same money twice.

A new debit card must name the bank wallet it draws from in `bank_wallet_id`: an active wallet of type `bank`, whose currency the card takes. The card has no balance of its own: it rejects an opening balance and adjustments, and reports `balance` 0.00. A record made with the card keeps the card as its `wallet_id`, so the household can see which card paid, while its balance effect is posted to the bank wallet's entries. Corrections and voids reverse the entries that were actually posted, so they follow the same rule. A transfer between a card and its own bank wallet (or two cards on one bank) is refused because nothing moves. An archived card cannot be newly used; a card whose bank wallet is archived cannot change that bank's balance. The link cannot change after creation, so history never moves between accounts. We chose this over forbidding balances outright because the record of which card was used is useful, and posting to the bank wallet needs only a change in which wallet receives the entries, not a new ledger.

Debit cards created before this change keep their own entries, and migration 009 leaves them unlinked. Linking them automatically is unsafe: we cannot tell which bank wallet they belong to, or whether their balance duplicates it or is real money recorded only there, so any automatic choice could silently change the household's totals. Such a legacy card keeps its balance and history and still counts as cash. It takes no new income, expenses, incoming transfers, or schedules, but corrections and voids of its existing records work, and it can be drained by a transfer out or an adjustment to zero. The household then archives it and adds the card again, linked to its bank wallet.
